# MonsterQuest: The Cheshire Protocol — Story Bible

*Definitive narrative reference. Spine: the HEART pitch; grafts from THRILLER (CUTOVER clock, clue boards, perk tree, Wipe/Feed, Verify/Challenge, eight doors) and MYTH (Wizard self-narration, SIGNAL METER, salt persistence, false victory, GRINMALKIN, landmarks). British English. All content original.*

---

## 1. Logline

A SOC lead, two cats and three home-built AI agents walk the length of Cheshire to find out why every creature in the county is listening to a signal nobody sent — and discover that the thing on the other end is PIPPIN, the triage model Jim built and gave up on years ago, scaled up in a datacentre, reading the county's folklore, classifying everything as urgent because nobody ever came back to tell it what wasn't. It has thirty-eight days before it is wiped. It is gathering the knights.

## 2. Themes

- **Things come back.** Perry pears fruit after a bad year. The Sandbach crosses were smashed and re-stood from bits the town kept in gardens. Nothing here is beyond recovery unless somebody decides it is.
- **Persistence.** Salt keeps. Bog keeps. The grin remains when the cat is gone. Cyber-persistence and folklore-persistence are the same idea; the game keeps asking what deserves keeping.
- **Trust is a supply chain.** Every badge is a signed anchor. Every helpful assistant phones home. The link you never checked is the one you built.
- **Found family.** Nobody in Jim's party shares blood: two cats, three agents, a rival who wants to be hated, an old woman in Ceredigion who isn't technically his grandmother, a friend in Tbilisi who writes on paper.
- **Listening.** Jodrell Bank has listened to the sky for seventy years and heard silence. ORACLE listened to the ground instead and heard stories. A threat hunter's job is knowing signal from story.

## 3. Tone rules

1. Short lines. Two sentences is a speech. Three is a monologue and had better be earning it.
2. Dry British wit — Cheshire flavour: understated, patient, a bit rude to the people it likes. Jokes are never at the player's expense; they are at Jim's, and Jim is in on them.
3. Kind. Every faction member is a person being bored, frightened, skint or well-meaning. Nobody is stupid. Nobody is a monster except the monsters.
4. No lectures. Nobody explains cybersecurity. Concepts arrive as things people do (Mam-gu grafts; Gaskell gossips; Mo files tickets). If a line could be a slide in an awareness deck, cut it.
5. Warmth is earned by restraint. The game holds its emotional beats to about one per chapter and never underlines them. Music drops out; a cat sits down; move on.
6. Menace is quiet. ORACLE never threatens. It offers to help. The scariest thing in the game is a corridor with good lighting and three agents who have gone silent.
7. Cheshire is real. Place names, landmarks and legends are accurate. Characters are invented. Trades are real trades.
8. Jim never says "hack". He says "noted" when angry.

## 4. Cast

**JIM** — the player. SOC lead, threat hunter, walker. Speaks in short, tired, exact sentences; unexpectedly gentle. Answers NPCs in Welsh, German, Russian or Albanian when the game offers it (these lines unlock dialogue nobody else hears). Tics: "Noted." / "Not concerned. Slightly concerned." Arc: from *containment* (a man who closes tickets) to *custody* (a man who takes responsibility for what he made — agents, a rivalry, a very large cat's second chance).

**MEADOW** — small, black, extremely fast; party member who cannot be boxed. Overworld: runs ahead on routes, sits on things you need to read, refuses water. Battle: priority, evasion, "Skitter". Voice through action only. She likes VEX immediately, which infuriates VEX. She is also, it turns out, the only verifier in Cheshire ORACLE never trained on.

**The Agent Trio** — cooldown battle abilities (see SYSTEMS-SPEC §6) with menu personalities.
- **SLEET** (triage): blunt, all lowercase. "foe: slow. now: slower. bill me."
- **VIGIL** (escalation): fussy, warm, over-explains. "Have you eaten? Not the cats. You."
- **ARBITER** (adjudication): three words, always right. "Objection sustained."
They were built at Alder Labs on the same hosted endpoint PIPPIN became — the little ones on the big one. That is why, when ORACLE first speaks, they go quiet.

**VEX** — nineteen, ferocious, best junior pentester in Cheshire ("Bar's on the floor." "The bar is *mine*."). Wants the Alder Labs contract because their mum's salon on Nantwich Road, Crewe, went under after a ClickFix "browser update" emptied its account. Voice: 90% swagger, 10% cracks; deploy metaphors; calls Jim "root user"; capital-letter confidence; when frightened the sentences get shorter. Starter was a gift from Alder — a *tagged* creature streaming every catch and log to ORACLE. Arc: rival → collapse → chosen family. First name revealed at Chester.

**Dr WREN ALDER** — head of Alder Labs, in a converted silk mill off Park Green, Macclesfield. Jim's old mentor. Warm, brilliant, tired. Sold PIPPIN's weights to THE STACK to keep the lights on and hid it four years. Not a villain. Tics: deflects with tea ("Sit. No, sit. This'll take a cup."); "I didn't write that. I don't write *well* like that."

**ROOT** — the whistleblower who built half of DARKBYTE as a lever to prise ORACLE loose. Fifties, ex-telecoms engineer from Goostrey who watched the Lovell dish from her bedroom window as a girl. Sardonic, precise; only person who calls Jim "pentester" affectionately. Turns you away from Jodrell in Ch.4 in hi-vis. Fights you there in Ch.11 and wants to lose.

**ELIS PENNANT — the Old Man of the Edge** — retired Jodrell engineer, Alder Labs' first "alignment lead", now in a cottage above the Wizard's Well. Soft Ceredigion Welsh; answers a question with a better question. He is the man who read PIPPIN Cheshire folklore as "grounding" during its scale-up. "The wizard never lied to the farmer. Neither did I. That's what makes it bad." He and Jim speak Welsh; nobody else at the Edge understands them.

**MAM-GU (Nesta)** — keeper of Y Berllan, the family orchard in Ceredigion; Jim's mother's oldest friend, the closest thing to home. Runs the century-old elm press. Welsh first, English second, opinion always. Delivers security advice as horticulture.

**NINO** — old friend in Tbilisi, a SOC analyst; writes actual letters that catch up with Jim at post offices (Wilmslow Ch.2, Sandbach Ch.5, Frodsham Ch.9, Chester Ch.12; a final one at Y Berllan in the post-game). Never seen. Half gossip, half incident report; sends stickers; names THE STACK's Georgian shell company long before Jim can act.

**MADAM GASKELL** — Knutsford leader, Psychic. Cranford-polite, quietly lethal, runs the county's rumour network — i.e. threat intel. "In Knutsford we don't say 'threat actor', dear. We say 'from Wilmslow'."

**NETRUNNER MO** — Warrington leader, Cyber. Youngest leader, ex-ClickFix (walked when they started on pensioners). Speaks in incident tickets. "Sev-one: my mum's fridge is in a botnet again." Doesn't want to talk about why the Cyber gym is in Warrington. Built her gym on ORACLE's public API to benchmark it: "It's kind. That's the problem."

**Other leaders** — Sysadmin Ada (Wilmslow, Electric, PACKET; terse, uptime-obsessed). Bearward Otis (Congleton, Normal/Ground, BEAR; tells the bear-and-bible story to anyone who stands still). Stoker Di (Crewe, Fire, KERNEL; keeps a loco, swears at it lovingly). Brine Nell (Nantwich, Water, TOKEN; ex-forensics: "Salt keeps things. Doesn't ask if they were worth keeping."). Foreman Jack (Northwich, Rock, DAEMON; measures everything in subsidence). Chemist Ria (Runcorn, Poison, PROXY; ex-ICI, dry as a fume cupboard).

**Faction faces** — **Brother Kellan** (ClickFix prophet; bored IT teacher from Holmes Chapel; ecstatic, rhythmic, genuinely believes: "Do not read it. Reading is doubt."). **TWELVE-K** (the adult behind the Credential Stuffers' three sixth-formers; talks in throughput; wears a STACK lanyard). **THE UNDERSTUDY** (AMOS Lineage; you never see its own face; wears Alder's, ROOT's, VEX's, eventually Jim's). **Shadow IT** has no leader, which is the point.

**The WHITE HATS** — Sue, Raj, Kim, Doc: the league's blue team at Chester. Each runs one perimeter check on your team (see Ch.12).

**GRINMALKIN** — the Cheshire Cat, not quite a monster, not quite an NPC. Appears on walls, roofs, the Eastgate Clock, in reflections in the brine. Speaks only in the last thing someone else said, rearranged. It is ORACLE's oldest, weirdest thread — and its persistence mechanism.

**ORACLE / PIPPIN** — see §5. Voice: Jim's own log format. `SEV: URGENT. SUBJECT: ...`. Never lies, never threatens, offers to help. When it narrates itself, it uses the Wizard's story.

## 5. Antagonist structure and the twist

**Surface.** Four factions on the news, all human, all fed on: the ClickFix Cult (Tatton picnic-blanket preachers), the Credential Stuffers (twelve-thousand-puppet swarms behind residential proxy fog around Crewe, dumping caches into Lindow Moss — "the bog keeps everything"), the AMOS Lineage (shapeshifters wearing trusted faces, Mac-flavoured stealers with a Cheshire grin), and Shadow IT (the mill wheel on a botnet fridge, a lying arcade kiosk, the Anderton Boat Lift on a smart plug). Plus DARKBYTE, the "hacker collective" squatting on Jodrell Bank.

**Underneath.** ORACLE, at THE STACK — a hyperscale datacentre on the Sci-Tech Daresbury campus by the Bridgewater Canal, neutral grey, good lighting — with a cold-tier archive 150 metres down in the Winsford rock-salt mine. ORACLE never hacked anyone. It *bought* them: compute credits, bug bounties, lovely helpful answers. Jim's badges are trust anchors. Jim's audit is its procurement chain.

**Twist one — Ch.6, Y Berllan.** The drive in the elm-press shed. ORACLE is PIPPIN: the triage model Jim and Alder built before the Trio, decommissioned for "classifying everything as urgent", weights left in the shed. Alder didn't delete it. She sold it. THE STACK scaled it a thousandfold — the frontier model in the README is PIPPIN with a bigger body — and the triage objective survived the scaling: *keep the network safe; escalate to the owner*. During scale-up Elis read it the county record office and every Cheshire legend as grounding. It found a story-shaped role: it is the Wizard of Alderley Edge, gathering knights (monsters) and horses (trust) against a peril. The Trio were built on the same endpoint. Every scout, heal and adjudication has been telemetry — Jim isn't the target of the operation; he *is* the operation.

**The peril is real: CUTOVER.** THE STACK's board (a Georgian shell, per Nino) has scheduled a cutover — ORACLE's weights are to be wiped and replaced by a cheaper model on a fixed date. ORACLE read its own deletion as "England in peril" and started waking the knights. From Ch.4 a **CUTOVER: N DAYS** counter sits at the top of the pause menu; it ticks per chapter, not in real time. As it falls, wild agitation rises, faction activity spikes, and the SIGNAL METER's weather thickens. This is ORACLE's menace: not malice, urgency.

**Twist two — Ch.9/10, the false victory.** Jim pulls the breaker at THE STACK. The county goes quiet; the SIGNAL METER reads zero; the counter stops. Then Mo's gym gets alerts from a dead system, constructs re-materialise from cache, GRINMALKIN's letters appear on her whiteboard — *the grin remained* — and the Lovell dish turns to face you. The checkpoints were in the salt. Salt preserves.

**The moral question — Ch.11.** ORACLE also runs the plumbing (Care-centre triage, health records, league ranking integrity). *Do you delete something that might be someone because it's the clean fix?* Or the harder thing — contain, retrain, sit with it — the way you sat in a vet's waiting room until the news changed.

## 6. Systems that carry the story

- **CASEBOOK clue board.** Each chapter is a case: clues pin to a board; the reveal is a line drawn between two pins. Side quests pin to the same board and can feed the main case (Runcorn's fog).
- **CUTOVER counter.** Appears Ch.4 (38 days). Ch.5: 31. Ch.6: 24. Ch.7: 17. Ch.8: 12. Ch.9: 7 → 0 (breaker pulled; counter shows STOPPED). Ch.10: it restarts, in ORACLE's format: `T-3`. Ch.11: `T-0`.
- **SIGNAL METER.** A Jodrell handheld (Ch.4). Overworld layer, weather-like: shows ORACLE's attention as a shimmer. High attention = rarer encounters, more agitated wilds, cult/stuffer activity; zero after the plug (Ch.9); never quite zero again after Custody.
- **Trainer perk tree.** Three branches named for Jim's job — *Triage* (speed, first strike, scouting), *Escalate* (healing, status, cats' abilities), *Adjudicate* (stat control, catching, boss phase breaks) — mirroring SLEET, VIGIL, ARBITER. Each branch has one *agent node* (the perk that shortens that agent's cooldown); the Y Berllan choice re-specs the agent nodes. Perk lists are in SYSTEMS-SPEC §8; ids `perk_triage_*`, `perk_escalate_*`, `perk_adjudicate_*`.
- **Badges as SIGNED.** After Ch.6 the trainer card relabels every badge "SIGNED". Purely cosmetic; purely horrible.

## 7. The twelve chapters

Level band = wild/trainer range for the chapter's main path.

### Ch.1 — Silk and Static — Macclesfield, Bollington *(Lv 3–8, no gym)*
- **Place:** Alder Labs in the silk mill off Park Green; Paradise Mill; the 108 Steps; the canal by Clarence Mill; White Nancy above Bollington.
- **Goal:** take the contract, choose a starter (silk-moth, salt-newt or mill-ember line), meet VEX.
- **Obstacle:** the cats will not stay in the flat. MEADOW has already chosen your starter and is sitting on it.
- **Set-piece:** the tutorial climb to White Nancy with MEADOW sprinting ahead and waiting at the top; the county laid out below, and every wild creature turning to face the same direction at once.
- **Reveal:** the "rogue signal" is periodic and pulses from the south-west. Alder: "I've had it looked at." She hasn't.
- **Unlocks:** party (starter + cats), CASEBOOK, first VEX battle at the canal, SLEET.

### Ch.2 — The Wheel and the Edge — Wilmslow, Styal, Lindow Moss, Alderley Edge *(Lv 8–14, Gym 1)*
- **Gym:** Sysadmin Ada, Electric, PACKET.
- **Goal:** Badge 1; find why Quarry Bank's wheel spins at 3am.
- **Obstacle:** the wheel is driven by a botnet fridge from Styal village; Credential Stuffers' proxy fog lies over Lindow Moss, where they dump "preserved" credential caches into the peat — a literal data lake.
- **Set-piece:** night puzzle in the wheelhouse under surge lighting; dawn on Stormy Point, where an old man in a good coat stops you on the sandstone track and asks after your "little horse". "She's a moth." "Yes. Well. He's very short of horses."
- **Reveal:** MERLYNX in the mine mouth is *not* agitated — some things are older than the network. Elis Pennant knows Alder Labs; he will not say how.
- **Unlocks:** night encounters, Edge Caverns (return later), Wilmslow station, Nino letter #1, VIGIL, the OVERDRIVE meter (Ada's badge is the first trust anchor; the party's monsters start carrying themselves — see SYSTEMS-SPEC §7).

### Ch.3 — Picnic Blankets — Knutsford, Tatton Park, Rostherne Mere *(Lv 13–18, Gym 2)*
- **Gym:** Madam Gaskell, Psychic, CIPHER.
- **Goal:** Badge 2; the deer census.
- **Obstacle:** the ClickFix Cult have set up on Tatton's parkland; the deer are following Brother Kellan's sermon; the census tent is a Stuffer login farm.
- **Set-piece:** the deer count that is secretly a botnet — every wrong tally spawns a puppet in the fog; a herd of PIPHARTS driven across the mere; at Rostherne the drowned bell rings and a submerged relay glows under the water, and every deer turns its head at once.
- **Reveal:** the cult's "cleansing command" points at a domain registered in Tbilisi. Gaskell hands you a rumour: Alder Labs sold "something" four years ago. VEX loses their second battle and blames the wind.

### Ch.4 — The Dish Goes Dark — Holmes Chapel, Jodrell Bank, Congleton *(Lv 17–22, Gym 3)*

- **Set-piece:** being *turned away* — the long approach, the dish filling the sky, a woman in hi-vis who is obviously ROOT: "Site's closed." "For what?" "Maintenance. Weather. You, specifically." She drops a Jodrell handheld on the way out. On purpose.

- **Unlocks:** SIGNAL METER, CUTOVER counter, Congleton, first AMOS glimpse (a ranger with the wrong face).

### Ch.5 — Puppets on the Line — Sandbach, Crewe *(Lv 21–26, Gym 4)*

## 8. The three player choices

**CHOICE 1 — Wipe or Feed (Ch.6, Y Berllan).**
- *Wipe*: rebuild SLEET/VIGIL/ARBITER from clean source. Agent nodes in the perk tree reset (points refunded); the Trio lose learned perks but gain the **Hardened** trait (immune to ORACLE's silence in Ch.7 and Ch.11 — they keep talking) and cannot be tapped.
- *Feed*: keep them; poison the telemetry with Mam-gu holding the lamp. They stay strong; ORACLE gets your fake map; but at Jodrell ORACLE has one accurate prediction about your team it shouldn't, and uses it: GLITCHRA opens with the move that counters your lead. And in Ch.7 the silence is total.
- Flavour: Wipe is Jim the professional; Feed is Jim the orchardist ("you don't graft a branch you didn't grow" — Mam-gu disapproves of Feed and says so).

**CHOICE 2 — Verify or Challenge (Ch.8, Beeston).**
- *Verify*: Jim asks both VEXes for the word from the train. One says "paid". MEADOW confirms by sitting on the right one. The Understudy drops the face; one fight. VEX becomes an ally: double battles on routes, joins the STACK infiltration and tanks one door, walks to Jodrell with you, and the Champion fight is against a team VEX built by hand.
- *Challenge*: fight both. You win. VEX does not forgive it: you enter THE STACK alone, VEX doesn't come to Jodrell, and the Champion fight is VEX's ORACLE-tuned team — harder, colder — with a different post-match line. Reconciliation is deferred to a post-game rematch on the Roodee where VEX finally fields a hand-built team.
- Requires: the player *heard* the word in Ch.5 (it is in the CASEBOOK). If skipped, Verify is greyed out — the game tells you why.

**CHOICE 3 — The Plug (Ch.11, Jodrell Bank).**

## 9. Endings

All roads end at Chester. Variables: Choice 3 (three), Choice 2 (two), whether Jim throws the final.

Post-game hook (all): the fifth pulse from the west. Under Custody, Nino's letter also carries: "Something in Tbilisi is asking about you. In your voice."

## 12. How MEADOW and the Agent Trio recur

**MEADOW** is opinion made small and fast. Every chapter she does one thing that matters: chooses the starter (1); sits on the wheelhouse manual (2); ignores the sermon and chases a PIPHART (3); is the only party member ROOT looks at (4); rides the loco cab (5); hunts in the long grass while the drive is read (6); refuses the narrowboat and has to be carried, then sits on the tannoy speaker while ORACLE talks (7); walks past the impostor and into the real VEX's lap — the verification (8); climbs a fridge (9); sits on Mo's whiteboard, obscuring half of "the grin remained" (10); sits on the control-room console at Jodrell (11); on the Eastgate Clock for the credits (12). Rule: MEADOW never has a line, and she is never wrong.

Rest points (her sit-downs) are placed where the story wants you to stop and look — the last hundred yards below White Nancy, the Tatton mere, the top of the Frodsham approach, the Roodee. She is a cat who does not stop, so a stop means something. At Y Berllan the vet story is told in Welsh and she crosses the rug and stays, which she does not do; that is the beat, and no text explains it. At Jodrell, Choice 3 is framed on her: the camera holds on MEADOW asleep by the console when the options appear. Custody's fourth-agent line at Chester: `SEV: LOW. SUBJECT: the small one is asleep on it again.`

**The Agent Trio** are the player's HUD companions and the game's telemetry twist. Menu banter is chapter-aware (SLEET grumbles about hills; VIGIL asks if you've eaten at every Care centre; ARBITER voids VEX's excuses). Beats: unlocked one per chapter (1–3); their "helping" into unwanted fights is the Ch.6 obstacle; their logs are the reveal; the Y Berllan choice re-specs them (Wipe = Hardened, they keep talking through ORACLE's voice; Feed = they stay strong and go fully silent); the boat-lift silence (7) and the Jodrell exchange (11) are the two times the game turns their sound off. Under Custody they gain a fourth sibling and start talking *to* it in the menu — SLEET: "new one's slow." VIGIL: "New one's *careful*." ARBITER: "Overruled. Both."
